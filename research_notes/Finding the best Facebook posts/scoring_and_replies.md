# Scoring Facebook/Nextdoor recommendation-request posts and replying so a lawn/landscaping company gets hired (Harford County, MD, 2025-2026)

Research scope note: Reddit could not be searched or fetched (the domain is blocked to the research tool), and LawnSite thread bodies are behind a paywall (HTTP 402). Contractor-forum evidence is therefore limited to search snippets. Nextdoor help-center pages returned 503 errors, so Nextdoor policy details come from Nextdoor's own blog and from search-result summaries of help-center pages.

## 1. How to score a recommendation-request post (quality signals)

### Takeaway
No published study scores Facebook or Nextdoor recommendation posts. The scoring model below is built from general lead-response research, a 2025 homeowner survey and platform rules. The strongest signals are post age (freshness), a named service with dated urgency, an address inside the service area, and how many competitors are already in the thread.

### Cited Findings
- Thumbtack-style shared leads go to several pros at once, and "the homeowner often hires whoever responds first." Contact rate is said to fall from 90%+ under 5 minutes to 60-70% at 5-15 minutes and 20-30% at 30 minutes. These decay numbers are from a vendor blog and have no clear primary source. — [NextPhone blog, Sept 2026](https://getnextphone.com/blog/paid-lead-response-time)
- The same vendor blog claims, without a source, that lead aggregators send each lead to "3-5 contractors" and that contractors close "approximately 13-18%" of shared HomeAdvisor leads. — [NextPhone](https://getnextphone.com/blog/paid-lead-response-time)
- Housecall Pro's 2025 survey of 1,000+ US homeowners: 52% judge contractor quality by online reviews, 38% by prior experience and 33% by a friend's recommendation. 97% say transparent pricing matters. — [Housecall Pro customer service report](https://www.housecallpro.com/resources/home-service-customer-service-report-trends-statistics/) (figures as summarized in search results)
- 59.2% of homeowners rely on trusted connections: 33.6% rehire someone they have used before and 25.6% hire on a personal recommendation. — [Mantel Homeowner Shopping Attitudes Report 2025](https://www.usemantel.com/blog/homeowner-shopping-attitudes-report-2025) (as summarized in search results)
- On Nextdoor, recommendations make up "nearly one-third of conversations among neighbors." — [Nextdoor business blog / help, via search summary](https://business.nextdoor.com/en-us/blog/how-businesses-can-get-more-recommendations-on-nextdoor)
- LawnSite users report that some Nextdoor posters are "constant complainers," and one user never got a customer in 5+ years of posting on Nextdoor. Others got regular customers from local Facebook "swip swap" groups. — [LawnSite thread on Nextdoor](https://www.lawnsite.com/threads/anybody-on-their-local-nextdoor-neighborhood-app.477946/) (search snippet only; thread body paywalled)

### Inferences (proposed scoring rubric, not tested)
- **Post age** (highest weight): under 30 min is hot, 30 min-3 h is warm, 3-24 h is low, over 24 h is skip unless no one has been tagged or the poster asked again. This follows the lead-response decay findings in section 2.
- **Competitors already in the thread**: 0-2 business replies or tags is high value. 3-8 is medium, so you need a distinctive reply. 10+ is low. If one company is already tagged repeatedly by neighbors with comments like "+1, they're great," treat the job as close to decided.
- **Poster engagement**: if the poster already replied "messaged you" or "thanks, booked" to someone, the post is likely dead. If the poster is still asking follow-up questions such as "anyone do mulch too?", it is open.
- **Service specificity and urgency**: a named service plus a date or trigger ("need spring cleanup + mulch before graduation party May 18," "grass is knee high, HOA letter") scores high. A vague "who does your lawn?" is a lower-intent survey, but it is still worth a reply for recurring mowing, which has high lifetime value.
- **Quote request vs. "who do you use"**: "Looking for quotes" or "need someone this week" signals active buying. "Who do you use / any recommendations" is often research. Recurring service (weekly mowing) is still valuable because one win becomes a season-long account.
- **Location**: require a town or neighborhood inside the service radius (Bel Air, Abingdon, Fallston, Forest Hill, Jarrettsville, Edgewood, Joppa, Havre de Grace, Aberdeen, Churchville). Score down if the post is outside the radius or is in a regional group with no location given.
- **Property/budget signals**: mentions of acreage, "large yard," new construction, HOA, a full landscape bed redo or a hardscape add-on score higher. Phrases like "cheap," "my budget is $25," or "teenager to mow" signal price shoppers. Score these lower unless you run a low-cost crew.
- **Homeowner vs. renter vs. property manager**: a homeowner is best for recurring work. A renter often does not control the lawn contract (landlord pays), so score lower. A property manager or realtor ("need 3 rentals cleaned up before listing") can be worth more because of repeat or multi-property volume. Ask for these posts in DM.
- **Platform fit**: Nextdoor posts come from verified local addresses, which is a strong location signal. Facebook local groups vary, and buy/sell/"swip swap" groups have produced customers for some operators according to the LawnSite snippet.

### Gaps
- No empirical data was found on hire rates by post age, comment count, or position of a reply in Facebook or Nextdoor threads specifically.
- No data was found on renter vs. owner conversion on social platforms.
- Reddit (r/lawncare, r/sweatystartup) anecdotes could not be retrieved.

## 2. Speed, time of day, and seasonality

### Takeaway
Evidence on general lead response, mostly older and not specific to social media, consistently shows that the first responder wins most of the time and that results drop sharply after 5 minutes and again after 1 hour. Spring (March-May) is the peak for landscaping requests, fall (October-November) is a secondary peak, and December-February is the low season. No data was found on the best hour or weekday for Facebook or Nextdoor landscaping posts.

### Cited Findings
- HBR, "The Short Life of Online Sales Leads" (2011; **older data**) studied 1.25M leads at 42 US companies. Firms that contacted a lead within an hour were about 7x as likely to qualify it as firms that waited longer, and 60x as likely as firms that waited 24+ hours. Average response time among 2,241 audited firms was 42 hours, and 23% never responded. — [summarized by CrankWheel](https://crankwheel.com/how-to-massively-increase-the-chance-of-qualifying-sales-leads/); original HBR article, Oldroyd, McElheran & Elkington, March 2011
- The "Lead Response Management Study" (Oldroyd/InsideSales, about 2007-2011; **older data, frequently re-cited without a date**) found 8x higher conversion within 5 minutes and 21x more qualified conversations at 5 vs. 30 minutes. The widely repeated "78% buy from the first company to respond" is also attributed to this research stream. — [NextPhone, 2026](https://getnextphone.com/blog/paid-lead-response-time); [Speed to lead roundup](https://www.getnextphone.com/blog/speed-to-lead)
- Chili Piper 2025 benchmark of 4M form submissions: instant response produced a 66.7% meeting-booking rate vs. 30% for standard follow-up (B2B forms, not home services). — [cited in search summary of Chiirp / speed-to-lead blogs](https://chiirp.com/insider_information_blog/the-real-cost-of-slow-lead-response)
- 97% of homeowners say an immediate response influences who they hire (2025). — [Housecall Pro report](https://www.housecallpro.com/resources/home-service-customer-service-report-trends-statistics/)
- Thumbtack Community threads show pros discussing the platform's "1 hour response time" norm and that customers are "significantly more likely to hire" when they hear back in the first hour. — [Thumbtack Community](https://community.thumbtack.com/discussion/comment/1513/)
- LocaliQ (April 2024-March 2025, 3,200+ home-service search campaigns): cost per lead rose for 69% of home-service businesses, by an average of +10.51% YoY. — [cited in speed-to-lead search summary](https://www.getnextphone.com/blog/speed-to-lead)
- Seasonality (Nov 2025 guide): spring lead volume runs 200-400% above the winter baseline in peak weeks. The guide's suggested budget weights are March-April 120-150%, May 150-180% (peak), June-July 80-100%, August-September 100-120%, October-November 120-140% (fall peak) and December 30-50%. — [LeadGen Economy 2026 landscaping guide](https://www.leadgen-economy.com/blog/landscaping-lawn-care-lead-generation-strategy-guide/)
- Spring work (cleanups, mulch, pruning, fertilization) can run into July before slowing in August. Fall cleanup and leaf removal form the second peak. Winter is mostly snow removal. — [Total Landscape Care](https://www.totallandscapecare.com/landscaping-blog/seasonal-landscaping-advantages/)

### Inferences
- Treat a fresh Facebook or Nextdoor recommendation post like a shared lead sent to every business in the group. Aim to reply within 5-15 minutes, and certainly within the first hour. Set group notifications and Nextdoor keyword alerts ("lawn," "mulch," "landscaper," "leaves," "snow") during March-May and October-November.
- For Harford County, expect clusters of mowing and spring-cleanup posts in March-April, mulch posts in April-May, leaf-cleanup posts from late October to early December, and snow posts right before forecast storms. This is inferred from the national patterns above. No Maryland-specific data was found.
- Evenings and weekends are plausibly when homeowners post (people see their yard after work and on Saturdays), but this is **unverified**. Log your own post timestamps for one season to confirm.

### Gaps
- No study was found of response speed in social-media comment threads specifically, as opposed to form or phone leads.
- No data was found on the best day or hour for landscaping recommendation posts on Facebook or Nextdoor.
- The "only 0.1% of leads get a response within 5 minutes" figure appears on vendor blogs without a traceable primary source.

## 3. Reply wording, account type, and what gets the poster to message or hire

### Takeaway
On Nextdoor as of August 2026, a business must reply from its Business Page, because promoting from a personal account is prohibited and auto-suppressed. On Facebook, replying as the Page is possible when the group allows Pages. Replies that work are short, local, disclose that you own the business, name the specific service, show proof (photos, reviews) and give one easy next step. No controlled study of reply wording was found.

### Cited Findings
- **Nextdoor policy change (August 19, 2026):** "Promoting a business, service, or paid opportunity can still happen on Nextdoor but it must be done from a free Business Page." The ban covers promotional "posts and comments in the main feed, as well as unsolicited direct messages" from personal accounts. Enforcement is pattern-based: flagged content stays visible but reaches fewer people. — [Nextdoor blog: self-promotion update](https://blog.nextdoor.com/self-promotion-update)
- Nextdoor's help center (via search summary) says self-promotion covers "posts, comments, and direct messages intended to acquire customers." Repeat violations can restrict the account. The detection system "look[s] at patterns of activity, not just who owns what," so the same business mentioned again and again from one personal account can match the pattern. — [Nextdoor Help: Self-Promotion using your Personal Account](https://help.nextdoor.com/s/article/Self-Promotion-using-your-Personal-Account?language=en_US); [FAQs About Our New Self-Promotion Policy](https://help.nextdoor.com/s/article/FAQs-About-Our-New-Self-Promotion-Policy?language=en_US)
- Earlier Nextdoor guidance (pre-2026): owners could reply to recommendation requests but had to disclose that they own the business, and could list the business in their personal profile bio. Friends and family who mention the business must also disclose the relationship. — [Podium: Nextdoor reviews for home service pros](https://www.podium.com/online-reviews/nextdoor-reviews/); [PetSit](https://www.petsit.com/advertising-on-nextdoor)
- Nextdoor asks businesses to have customers "tag your Business Page in their replies to neighbours who are looking for recommendations." Pages with recommendations get "up to 19X more views," and 72% of neighbors say recommendations influence them. — [Nextdoor small business guide](https://business.nextdoor.com/en-gb/small-business/resources/blog/small-business-guide-to-getting-nextdoor-recommendations)
- When your Business Page is tagged, Nextdoor advises joining the conversation, thanking the neighbor and inviting a recommendation on your Page. — [Nextdoor help/blog via search summary](https://help.nextdoor.com/s/article/recommendations-faq?language=en_US)
- **Facebook:** Pages can join groups and comment as the Page when the group admin allows it ("Allow Pages to request to join" is on by default). You choose personal profile or Page when joining. — [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/facebook-will-now-allow-pages-to-join-facebook-groups/532770/); [Agorapulse](https://www.agorapulse.com/blog/comment-as-page-on-facebook/)
- Facebook displays a Page's typical response time, and users "expect quick, helpful responses." Contractors are advised to ask happy customers to post photos in local groups and tag the company, sometimes with a discount incentive. — [Unilock Contractors blog](https://contractor.unilock.com/blog/facebook-groups-the-modern-word-of-mouth-referral/) (undated)
- Advice for Facebook groups: check each group's rules on self-promotion, answer lawn questions helpfully before selling, and when someone asks for a landscaper, "reply with a helpful comment and include your business details." — [MowMore](https://mowmore.com/blogs/news/how-to-market-your-landscaping-business-using-facebook-groups); [NiceJob](https://get.nicejob.com/resources/how-to-get-more-lawn-care-customers)
- Homeowner expectations (2025): 68% expect photos, video or a checklist of completed work. 97% want transparent pricing. 72% would pay 10% more for a pro with a better customer-service reputation. — [Housecall Pro report](https://www.housecallpro.com/resources/home-service-customer-service-report-trends-statistics/)
- Posts with pictures get higher reach and engagement than text-only posts. — [NiceJob](https://get.nicejob.com/resources/how-to-get-more-lawn-care-customers)

### Inferences (reply templates, not tested)
- **Strong reply (Facebook, as Page or as owner with disclosure):** "Hi Sarah, I'm Mike, owner of [Company]. We're based in Bel Air and do spring cleanups and mulch around Forest Hill every week. We can usually get out within 3-4 days for a free estimate. Here are a couple of beds we did last month [2 before/after photos]. Text me at 410-xxx-xxxx or I'll DM you. 4.9★ on Google (120 reviews)."
  - Why it works: it uses her name, discloses ownership, signals you are local, names her exact service, gives a timeline, shows proof (the 2025 survey says homeowners expect photos and reviews) and offers one clear next step (phone plus DM). It is short enough to read on a phone.
- **Weak replies:** "PM sent" or "Check your inbox" (unknown company, looks like spam, and unsolicited DMs are banned on Nextdoor); a pasted wall of text listing every service; a link only; "We're the best and cheapest!"; any reply that skips the service the poster asked about.
- Answer any specific question in the post publicly (e.g. "Do you haul away debris? Yes, included."). It shows competence to every reader in the thread.
- Give the phone number publicly and also offer to DM. Different people prefer to call, text or message, and a public number removes a step.
- On Nextdoor, reply only from the Business Page. Do not DM unless the poster invites it (for example, "send me info").

### Gaps
- No A/B or controlled data was found comparing short vs. long replies, phone number vs. "DM me," or Page vs. personal profile on Facebook.
- No data was found on whether the Facebook group algorithm ranks Page comments lower than personal-profile comments.

## 4. Follow-up: DMs, friend requests, booking links

### Takeaway
Follow up in DM on Facebook only after a public comment, and ideally only after the poster engages or invites contact. On Nextdoor, unsolicited promotional DMs from personal accounts are now explicitly prohibited. No evidence was found for or against friend requests. Fast follow-up after contact matters a great deal.

### Cited Findings
- Nextdoor (August 2026) prohibits "unsolicited direct messages" used for promotion from personal accounts. — [Nextdoor blog](https://blog.nextdoor.com/self-promotion-update)
- Neighbors can message a Business Page directly, and businesses can view and reply. — [Nextdoor help via search summary](https://help.nextdoor.com/s/article/About-business-posts-for-neighbors?language=en_US)
- 85% of callers who do not reach someone will not call back (attributed to a 2025 CallRail report). — [NextPhone](https://getnextphone.com/blog/paid-lead-response-time) (secondary citation)

### Inferences
- **Facebook sequence:** comment publicly, then send one short DM ("Hi Sarah, Mike from [Company], saw your post about mulch in Forest Hill. Happy to swing by Thursday for a free estimate. Here's a link to pick a time: [booking link], or text 410-xxx-xxxx.") After that, follow up once at about 24-48 h if there is no reply, then stop.
- Facebook DMs from non-friends land in "Message requests" and may never be seen. That is one reason to make sure the public comment carries the phone number. This is a widely known platform behavior but was not verified in this research.
- Avoid friend requests from a business owner's profile. They feel pushy to many people and add nothing a Page or phone number does not.
- Use a booking or estimate link (Jobber, Housecall Pro request forms, etc.) as the DM call to action, and answer missed calls or texts immediately.

### Gaps
- No data was found on Facebook DM vs. comment-only conversion, or on the effect of friend requests.

## 5. Commenting from multiple team members' accounts

### Takeaway
This is risky and is likely to hurt more than help. On Nextdoor it breaks the August 2026 self-promotion rules and the disclosure requirement, and pattern detection is designed to catch it. On Facebook it risks Meta's inauthentic-behavior and spam enforcement and group-admin bans. Neighbors also see through it.

### Cited Findings
- Meta's Community Standards prohibit misrepresenting yourself, fake accounts and "artificially boost[ing] the popularity of content." Coordinated inauthentic networks are banned. — [Meta Transparency Center: Inauthentic Behavior](https://transparency.meta.com/policies/community-standards/inauthentic-behavior)
- In H1 2025, Meta removed about 10M impersonating profiles and acted against about 500K accounts for spam or fake engagement, including "repetitive comments," with penalties such as comment demotion and reduced distribution. — [Fox News](https://www.foxnews.com/tech/meta-deletes-10-million-facebook-accounts-year-why.amp); [BrandCom](https://brandcom.ng/2025/07/17/meta-deletes-10-million-fake-profiles-over-impersonation-fake-engagement/)
- Facebook comment tools can now auto-hide comments from suspected fake accounts. — [BrandCom / search summary](https://brandcom.ng/2025/07/17/meta-deletes-10-million-fake-profiles-over-impersonation-fake-engagement/)
- Nextdoor: friends and family who mention a business must disclose the relationship, and the detection system flags the same business recurring from personal accounts "even when there's no connection." — [Podium](https://www.podium.com/online-reviews/nextdoor-reviews/); [Nextdoor Help](https://help.nextdoor.com/s/article/Self-Promotion-using-your-Personal-Account?language=en_US)
- Nextdoor states that "on Nextdoor it's supposed to be neighbors recommending your business, not you promoting it" (LawnSite user paraphrase). — [LawnSite](https://www.lawnsite.com/threads/most-effective-types-of-marketing-for-a-local-landscaper.484894/) (snippet)

### Inferences
- **Acceptable:** one company voice per thread (the Page, or the owner with clear disclosure), and real customers tagging you, which Nextdoor explicitly encourages. An employee who lives in the neighborhood may mention the company if they disclose "I work for them."
- **Not acceptable:** several staff accounts posting "+1, they're great!" without disclosure. This is astroturfing, and on Facebook it may trigger admin removal or spam demotion.

### Gaps
- No case data was found on businesses penalized or banned specifically for multi-account commenting in local groups.
