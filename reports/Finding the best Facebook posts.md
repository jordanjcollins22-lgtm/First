# Win Harford County jobs from local groups

The best Facebook posts to answer are in a small set of town and neighborhood groups, not the home feed. JS Landscaping's own numbers make the case: **172 home-feed posts produced 1 request, while 9 local groups produced most of the 163 requests** in the same 10 days. That is first-party evidence, and it is stronger than anything published, because **no public study ranks group types or measures hire rates on Facebook or Nextdoor posts**. The practical playbook has four parts. First, aim the extension at per-group "Most Recent" searches in those 9 groups plus a few new ones. Second, score each post on age, competitors already in the thread, service fit and location. Third, reply within about 15 minutes with one disclosed, specific owner comment. Fourth, stop sending several team or affiliate accounts into the same thread. That last practice is the biggest risk in the current setup: FTC rules require employees and affiliates to disclose the connection in the comment itself, with **penalties up to $53,088 per violation** under the 2024 reviews rule, and Meta and Nextdoor both police repetitive and inauthentic engagement. On Facebook's terms: Meta bars any automated collection of data, logged-in users included, and no sanctioned API has existed since **April 22, 2024**. For a single business the realistic consequence is account restriction, not a lawsuit. Throughout this report, **Evidence** marks a cited finding and **Inference** marks a recommendation built from evidence that is not about this exact situation.

## Your 9 groups already outperform the feed by roughly 100 to 1

**Evidence (first-party):** the home feed gave 1 request in 172 posts (about 0.6%). The 9 configured groups gave most of 163 requests. **Evidence (published):** lawn-care marketing guidance agrees on where requests appear, "local community groups, home improvement forums, or local buy/sell groups" ([MowMore](https://mowmore.com/blogs/news/how-to-market-your-landscaping-business-using-facebook-groups); [Jobber](https://getjobber.com/academy/lawn-care/how-to-get-lawn-care-customers-fast/)). LawnSite users report getting regular customers from local "swip swap" buy/sell groups, while one operator never landed a Nextdoor customer in 5+ years ([LawnSite](https://www.lawnsite.com/threads/anybody-on-their-local-nextdoor-neighborhood-app.477946/), snippet only). No source ranks moms vs. town vs. HOA vs. buy/sell groups. Web search also cannot list Harford County's private groups, because it surfaces only Pages such as The Harford County Mom and Hello Harford ([FB](https://www.facebook.com/theharfordcountymom/)).

**Inference:** drop the home-feed scroll, or cut it to a rare sanity check. It burns automated page loads (the activity Meta's terms prohibit) for almost no leads. To grow past 9 groups, use Facebook's own Groups search. Pair each town (Bel Air, Abingdon, Aberdeen, Fallston, Edgewood, Joppa, Forest Hill, "Harford County") with "community," "neighbors," "moms," "buy sell," "recommendations" or "what's happening." Keep a group when three things hold: it gets several local recommendation posts a week, its rules allow business replies when someone asks, and its members are homeowners. HOA and subdivision groups are small, but they put the right households in front of fewer competitors. Rank your current 9 groups by requests per week and by jobs actually booked, not by member count. The board already holds the data to do this: log group, post time, whether you replied, minutes to reply, and outcome. After one season, that log will be the only Harford-specific conversion data that exists anywhere.

## Group search is the reliable finder now that the API and "Posts" filter are gone

**Evidence:** Meta deprecated the Groups API in Graph API v19.0 and removed it, including `groups_access_member_info` and `publish_to_groups`, from all versions on **April 22, 2024** ([Ayrshare](https://www.ayrshare.com/blog/facebook-removes-groups-api-access-impact-and-implications/); [Meta developer thread](https://developers.facebook.com/community/threads/584177870580474/)). No sanctioned way remains to read group posts programmatically. Since about **September 2025**, many accounts have also lost the "Posts" filter in global search, with reports continuing through August 2026 and no fix ([PiunikaWeb](https://piunikaweb.com/2025/09/11/facebook-posts-search-filter-removed/); [iTechGuides](https://www.itechguides.com/facebooks-posts-search-filter-is-missing-for-many-users-what-still-works/)). Search inside a single group still works and offers **Most Recent**, Date (by year only), Posted By and Location filters ([Groupboss](https://groupboss.io/blog/search-facebook-group-posts/)). Ordinary members get no native keyword alerts. Only group admins can set Keyword Alerts, at `facebook.com/groups/[ID]/keyword_alerts` ([Groupboss](https://groupboss.io/blog/facebook-group-keyword-alerts/amp/); [OneStopSocial](https://onestopsocial.com/en/blog/facebook-group-monitoring-tools)).

Every commercial monitor works in one of two ways: it runs inside your logged-in browser, or it runs vendor-side accounts. Prices are as each vendor publishes them, and each vendor writes about its rivals.

| Tool | How it reads | Published price | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| OneStopSocial | Extension, your session | $29/mo, 25 groups | Email, browser or webhook alerts ([source](https://onestopsocial.com/en/blog/facebook-group-monitoring-tools)) |
| Devi AI / Syndr AI | Extension, your session | $49/mo, 25 groups | Syndr adds 15 subreddits ([Syndr](https://www.syndr.ai/facebook-group-monitoring-tool)) |
| Groups Watcher | Vendor cloud, no login | $99/mo, 25 groups | Sources conflict on whether it is now a managed service ([site](https://www.groupswatcher.com/)) |
| Leadhall | Vendor-side, FB + Nextdoor | From $99/mo | Under-5-minute alerts; lists landscapers ([site](https://leadhall.com/)) |

**Inference:** JS Landscaping's extension is already in the same class as OneStopSocial, Devi and Syndr. What paid tools add is scheduling, AI intent filtering and alert routing, and the sorter already does the filtering. The cheapest upgrade is to have the extension loop each of the 9+ groups' search with Most Recent on, over short phrase pairs, and push a phone notification for every new request. Pair a service word ("landscaper," "lawn guy," "mowing," "mulch," "leaf cleanup," "fall cleanup," "hedge," "aeration," "snow removal," "plow") with an intent word ("recommend," "anyone know," "looking for," "ISO," "need someone"). The pairing keeps out sellers of mowers and other contractors' ads. Ignore the year-level Date filter and use each post's own timestamp. Write the code to tolerate global search either showing or hiding the Posts filter. Two cheap additions need no automation at all. One is an admin-run group of your own (for example, a Harford County home-and-yard recommendations group), which is the only way to get native Keyword Alerts. The other is turning on "All posts" notifications for the top 3 to 5 groups. That setting is product knowledge, not confirmed by a source in this research. Before buying a vendor-side monitor such as Leadhall, ask the vendor to confirm it covers your specific private groups, because a monitor that "needs no login" can only see groups its own accounts belong to.

**Nextdoor and Reddit are secondary channels.** **Evidence:** Nextdoor's **August 19, 2026** policy says promotion "must be done from a free Business Page, not a personal neighbor account," in posts, comments and DMs. Flagged content gets reduced reach ([Nextdoor blog](https://blog.nextdoor.com/self-promotion-update)). User-review analysis reports a separate 2026 change in service categories, landscaping included: Business Pages reportedly have to pay to comment on neighbors' requests, at **$80 to $2,000+/mo** with no published rate card ([Unstar.app](https://unstar.app/blog/nextdoor-business-paywall-cant-comment-suspended-2026)). Nextdoor's own blog does not mention this, so treat it as unconfirmed for Harford County until you check in your own account. **Inference:** on Nextdoor, put the effort into getting past customers to recommend and tag your Business Page. Recommendations reportedly drive "up to 19X more views" ([Nextdoor Business](https://business.nextdoor.com/en-gb/small-business/resources/blog/small-business-guide-to-getting-nextdoor-recommendations)). Reply only from the Business Page. On Reddit, a free F5Bot alert limited to a local subreddit is enough ([F5Bot](https://f5bot.com/guides/reddit-keyword-alerts)). Reddit's API now needs manual approval under its November 2025 Responsible Builder Policy ([Data365](https://data365.co/blog/reddit-oauth-api-documentation)), which local Reddit volume does not justify.

## Score posts on freshness first, then crowding, fit and place

**Evidence:** no study measures hire rates by post age or comment count in social threads. The closest evidence is general lead-response research. HBR's analysis of 1.25M leads (2011, older) found that firms contacting a lead within an hour were about **7x** as likely to qualify it as firms that waited longer, and **60x** as likely as firms that waited 24+ hours ([CrankWheel summary](https://crankwheel.com/how-to-massively-increase-the-chance-of-qualifying-sales-leads/)). Housecall Pro's 2025 survey of 1,000+ homeowners found that **97%** say an immediate response influences who they hire. In the same survey, 52% judge quality by reviews and 68% expect photos or a record of completed work ([Housecall Pro](https://www.housecallpro.com/resources/home-service-customer-service-report-trends-statistics/)). The popular claim that "the first contractor to respond wins 78% of the time" comes from vendor marketing and traces back to old, undated research ([Leadhall](https://leadhall.com/); [NextPhone](https://getnextphone.com/blog/paid-lead-response-time)).

**Inference:** a recommendation post works like a shared lead sent to every contractor in the group. That makes the AI sorter's request/ad/other label only the first gate. Add a score the board can sort by. The weights below are untested and should be tuned against your booking log.

| Signal | High value | Low value / skip |
|---|---|---|
| Post age | Under 30 min; 30 min to 3 h still warm | Over 24 h, unless no one has answered or the poster asked again |
| Business replies already in thread | 0 to 2 | 10+, or one company tagged repeatedly with "+1 they're great" |
| Poster status | Still asking follow-ups ("anyone do mulch too?") | Already wrote "booked" or "messaged you" |
| Service and urgency | Named service plus a date or trigger ("mulch before party May 18," "HOA letter") | Vague "who does your lawn?" (still worth a reply for recurring mowing) |
| Location | Town or neighborhood inside the service area | Outside the area, or a regional group with no location |
| Job size | Large yard, acreage, new build, bed redo, recurring | "Cheap," "$25 budget," "teenager to mow" |
| Who's asking | Homeowner; property manager or realtor with several properties | Renter whose landlord controls the lawn |

**Seasonality.** **Evidence:** national landscaping lead volume peaks in March to May, with a second peak in October to November and lows in December, and spring weeks run 200 to 400% above the winter baseline ([LeadGen Economy](https://www.leadgen-economy.com/blog/landscaping-lawn-care-lead-generation-strategy-guide/); [Total Landscape Care](https://www.totallandscapecare.com/landscaping-blog/seasonal-landscaping-advantages/)). **Inference:** today is October 6, so leaf and fall-cleanup requests are about to surge in Harford County, and that surge should run into early December. Snow posts arrive in bursts just before forecast storms. During these windows, raise the extension's search frequency and staff the board so someone can reply within 15 minutes. That includes evenings and Saturdays, when homeowners plausibly post. The evening-and-Saturday pattern is unverified, and your timestamp log will confirm or refute it within a season.

## One disclosed owner reply beats a chorus of team comments

**Evidence:** no controlled study of reply wording exists. Homeowners want proof and transparency: 68% expect photos or records of work, and 97% value transparent pricing ([Housecall Pro](https://www.housecallpro.com/resources/home-service-customer-service-report-trends-statistics/)). Facebook Pages can join groups and comment as the Page where admins allow it ([Agorapulse](https://www.agorapulse.com/blog/comment-as-page-on-facebook/)). Practitioner guidance says to answer the request with "a helpful comment and include your business details" ([NiceJob](https://get.nicejob.com/resources/how-to-get-more-lawn-care-customers)).

**Inference, a template to adapt:** "Hi Sarah, I'm [name], owner of JS Landscaping. We're local to Bel Air and doing fall leaf cleanups in Abingdon this week. We can usually get out for a free estimate within a few days. Here are two yards we finished last week [before/after photos]. Text me at 410-xxx-xxxx or I can message you." It works because it uses the poster's name, discloses ownership, names her exact service and town, gives a timeline, shows proof, and offers one easy next step. Answer any question in the post publicly ("Yes, haul-away is included"). Put the phone number in the comment, because DMs from non-friends often sit unseen in Message Requests (platform knowledge, not verified here). Send at most one DM after the comment, with a booking link, and one follow-up at 24 to 48 hours. Avoid "PM sent," a bare link, "best and cheapest," and a pasted list of every service. Write each reply fresh, because identical text across groups is a known spam trigger ([Lilach Bullock](https://www.lilachbullock.com/stop-facebook-groups-removing-business-posts/)). If the board uses AI to draft replies, have it vary the wording by default, and have a person edit and send every one.

**The "Posts to Answer" board needs a rule change.** **Evidence:** the FTC's Endorsement Guides say employees must disclose the relationship in the post itself, because a profile listing is not enough ("people who just read what you post won't get that information"). Companies must also train and monitor the people who promote them ([FTC FAQs](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/ftcs-endorsement-guides)). The Consumer Reviews and Testimonials Rule has been in effect since **October 21, 2024**. It bars undisclosed testimonials from officers, managers, employees and agents and enables civil penalties up to $53,088 per violation, and the FTC sent warning letters in late 2025 ([Benesch, April 2026](https://www.beneschlaw.com/insight/five-stars-zero-tolerance-ftc-turns-up-enforcement-under-consumer-review-rule/)). Meta prohibits "artificially boost[ing] the popularity of content" ([Meta: Inauthentic Behavior](https://transparency.meta.com/policies/community-standards/inauthentic-behavior)). In H1 2025 Meta acted against about 500K accounts for spam or fake engagement, including "repetitive comments" ([Fox News](https://www.foxnews.com/tech/meta-deletes-10-million-facebook-accounts-year-why.amp)). Nextdoor's detection flags the same business recurring from personal accounts ([Nextdoor Help](https://help.nextdoor.com/s/article/Self-Promotion-using-your-Personal-Account?language=en_US)).

**Inference:** run the board as "one company voice per thread." Assign each post to exactly one responder: the owner, the Page, or one designated team member. That person writes "I'm the owner of" or "Full disclosure, I work for JS Landscaping" in the comment. Affiliates who are paid per referral count as material connections and must disclose the same way. They should comment only in groups where they really live. Never post "+1, they're great" from staff accounts. The board should record who replied and should block a second team member from claiming the same post. The highest-value multi-voice tactic is the legitimate one: real customers tagging you. Ask happy customers to post photos and tag the company ([Unilock](https://contractor.unilock.com/blog/facebook-groups-the-modern-word-of-mouth-referral/)).

## Group bans come from velocity, links and ignoring rules

**Evidence:** Meta publishes no fixed limits. Its spam standard targets engagement "at very high frequencies," and lower frequencies when other spam signals are present ([Meta: Spam](https://transparency.meta.com/policies/community-standards/spam/)). Practitioner guidance suggests 1 to 2 group joins a day for new accounts, 4 to 6 for established ones, and never more than 5 an hour, with business-linked profiles treated like new accounts. First blocks typically last 24 to 48 hours, and repeat blocks last about a week ([Lilach Bullock](https://www.lilachbullock.com/how-many-facebook-groups-can-you-join-without-getting-flagged/)). Local groups often hide a no-promotion rule in their membership questions, and admins filter phrases like "DM me" ([Lilach Bullock](https://www.lilachbullock.com/facebook-group-rules-before-you-post/)). The same source recommends contributing for two weeks before promoting, roughly 80% help to 20% promotion, and messaging admins about their policy ([Lilach Bullock](https://www.lilachbullock.com/stop-facebook-groups-removing-business-posts/)).

**Inference:** for each of the 9 groups, record its promotion rule on the board: business replies allowed, owner-only, tag-only, promo-day only, or none. Have the board warn before a reply goes into a restricted group. Message each admin once to ask what they allow, and ask whether they sell a "preferred vendor" spot. Keep links out of first comments. Space replies to a handful per hour per account. If a block appears, stop that account for the full period instead of retrying.

**Facebook's terms on automated reading.** **Evidence:** Meta's Terms say "You may not access or collect data from our Products using automated means (without our prior permission)" ([Meta Terms](https://www.facebook.com/terms.php)). The Automated Data Collection Terms require express written permission, bar transferring the data, and bar collecting personal data that isn't publicly available ([Meta ADC Terms](https://www.facebook.com/legal/automated_data_collection_terms)). In Meta v. Bright Data (2024), Meta lost only because the scraping was logged-off. The court said the terms exist to stop "account holders... from abusing their access" ([Farella Braun + Martel](https://www.fbm.com/post/102kqw7/)). Meta sued BrandTotal over browser extensions that collected data through users' logged-in access, and the case settled in 2022 with an injunction and a "significant" payment ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2022/10/03/meta-settles-lawsuit-for-significant-sum-against-businesses-scraping-facebook-and-instagram-data)). **Inference:** the extension breaches the Terms even at human pace. The exposure that matters for one in-house business is losing the owner's profile, which is both the data source and a commenting account. Litigation risk rises if you distribute the extension, aggregate data on a server, or keep commenters' personal details. To reduce risk:

1. Stop auto-scrolling the home feed.
2. Prefer parsing what a person has on screen over unattended scrolling.
3. Store only post text, URL, group and timestamp, not profiles.
4. Never automate commenting.
5. Run the extension on an account that isn't the business's only lifeline.

No source measures how often Meta catches read-only extensions, and vendor claims that extensions get accounts banned come from competitors.

## Conclusion

The research changes the question from "where are the posts?" to "how fast and how cleanly can we answer them?" JS Landscaping already knows where requests come from, and its 9-group yield is better local evidence than any published benchmark. Gains now come from three changes. Cut the low-yield home-feed scrolling. Rank posts by freshness and crowding, not just by the request label. Treat the 15-minute response window during fall cleanup and the first snows as an operational target. The current setup is weakest at the stage that should be strongest, the reply. Several personal accounts commenting without disclosure create FTC, Meta and group-admin exposure, and they also look less trustworthy to neighbors than one named owner with photos and a phone number.

Two unknowns deserve direct testing rather than more research. The first is whether Nextdoor's reported paywall on business comments applies in Harford County. The second is which of the 9 groups and which post types actually turn into booked jobs. A simple outcome column on the "Posts to Answer" board answers both within a season. It would also give the business the Harford County conversion data that no vendor or study currently has.
